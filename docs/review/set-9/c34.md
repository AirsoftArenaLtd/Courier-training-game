## Five-minute review

1. Watch the [24-second keyboard clearance strike](c34-final/c34-clearance-strike-review.webm).
   A safe halt earns its clearance check; subsequently moving off into the beam causes
   roof damage and a separate failed strike check. Holding into it cannot repeat either
   penalty. Initial travel placement is a fixture; the recorded drive uses actual keys.
2. Compare the [Low tire/side view](c34-release/c34-release-loaded-low-low-damageleft.png),
   [High tire/side view](c34-release/c34-release-loaded-high-high-damageleft.png), and
   [open cargo door](c34-release/c34-release-loaded-low-low-damagecargo.png). Scuffs, folded dents and
   major damage have geometry cues as well as colour. Rear wear follows the opening
   door; wheel wear stays on the tire, independently of body pitch/roll. Missing models
   retain the procedural van and its attached wear.
3. Check the [larger Tamil warning and gauges](c34-final/c34-low-font-ta-large-clearance.png),
   [High equivalent](c34-final/c34-high-font-ta-large-clearance.png), and
   [reverse warning proof](c34-release/c34-reverse-warning.json). The reverse check
   includes visible text, narration and the live camera. New English awaits translation.
4. A new marked day from day four can start with existing wear. That is the baseline,
   never a player impact. New damage occupies a separate seven-zone array. C-35 adds
   the inspection/report workflow; this branch does not prematurely grade missing reports.
5. The cargo view shows a bounded shift without changing any parcel or shelf identity.
   The satellite navigation excludes both low-clearance edges and retains a reachable
   detour to every delivery curb. First-three-day and old unmarked checkpoints retain
   their original behavior; trainer damage-off suppresses the new reports.

# C-26: visible weather

17 October 2026, `codex/c26-visible-weather`, updated onto pilot `492dcb1` after C-24/C-25 merged.
The weather pass adds a physical rain/wiper pane, wet sky/sun reflections, snow and ice surface cues,
depth mist, cold breath and tire spray. Existing weather rules, scores, controls and saves are retained.

Clock, travel positions, weather selection and the stationary viewpoints are fixtures. Cargo remains at
the depot in the visual palette fixtures; the CPU sweep and rendered benchmark load the van. Wiping
uses actual K input and the physical stalk selected through P/E. The comparison requires changed GL
pixels, saved switch restoration, zero extra score events and a clean shader/GL error state. Independent
surface comparisons disable only the weather uniforms and require visible differences for rain,
storm, fog, snow and ice. The clip advances mission time on a stationary van to show the wipe cycle.

The full cab/mirror/rear-camera/keyboard/camera/driving/route-day/guide suites also pass. Their existing
fixtures exercise real controls, scoring and resume separately from the weather palette. The merged
mirror/rear/lighting build, sync and disposal order requested by Claude has been checked and retained.

All text is existing translated interface text; no new on-screen strings or shared Settings, i18n,
save or scoring changes were added. The first clear morning retains its appearance and rules.
Reduced motion keeps rain/particle positions and ripples static while clearing remains functional.

Low's unchanged limits are 64 calls, 2,500 triangles, two Three.js textures, 4 MiB attachments and
4 MiB estimated Phaser textures. Soft particle quads recover triangle headroom; the physical glass
adds one main-view call and never enters a reflection pass. Fog remains 6–28 metres and storms
12–40 metres. Surface reflections use the current sky/sun without a new road render target.
Raw figures and commands are in `docs/PERF.md`; implementation details are in `docs/3D-WEATHER.md`.
The software renderer establishes resource/behaviour checks; the native Intel 60 fps check remains O-1.
